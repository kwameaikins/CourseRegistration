# IFRS 18: Preparing Your Financial Statements for 2027

**Course curriculum — v2 (two-day format)**

> *Your 2027 accounts begin with your 2026 numbers.*

---

## 1. Course overview

IFRS 18 is effective for annual reporting periods beginning on or after 1 January 2027, with earlier application permitted, and it replaces IAS 1. It applies retrospectively.

For an entity with a 31 December year end, the first IFRS 18 financial statements are FY2027 — and the comparative period is **FY2026, which is already running**. Entities that have not begun categorising income and expenses on an IFRS 18 basis will be reconstructing the comparative year backwards from records built on IAS 1 logic.

IFRS 18 does not change recognition or measurement. Profit is the same number. What changes is how performance is structured, subtotalled, disaggregated and explained — and for entities that rely on alternative performance measures, what must now be disclosed and reconciled.

### Course aim

To equip preparers, reviewers and auditors to produce and assess IFRS 18-compliant financial statements, and to complete a transition plan for their own entity before the comparative year closes.

### Learning outcomes

On completion, participants will be able to:

1. Explain what IFRS 18 changes and, equally important, what it does not.
2. Classify income and expenses into the five categories and present the two required subtotals.
3. Determine whether an entity has a specified main business activity and apply the consequent classification.
4. Identify management-defined performance measures and prepare the required note.
5. Apply the aggregation and disaggregation principles to primary statements and notes.
6. Apply the consequential amendments to IAS 7, IAS 8, IAS 33 and related standards.
7. Execute retrospective transition, including restatement of comparatives and the required reconciliation.
8. Produce a transition plan and timetable for their own entity.

### Target audience

Financial accountants and controllers, financial reporting managers, external and internal auditors, CFOs and finance directors, audit committee members, and preparers in regulated sectors. Particularly relevant to listed entities, banks, insurers and any entity that reports adjusted performance measures to lenders or investors.

**Prerequisites:** Working knowledge of IFRS financial statement preparation. Familiarity with IAS 1 assumed.

## 3. Module detail

### Module 1 — Why IFRS 18 exists, and what it does not change
**1 hour**

**Purpose:** Set the frame. Participants who arrive expecting accounting changes will hunt for them all course; establishing early that this is a presentation standard prevents that.

**Topics**
- The Primary Financial Statements project and the investor concerns behind it: incomparable operating profit, unexplained adjusted measures, unhelpful aggregation
- What IFRS 18 replaces and what it retains — the IASB did not reconsider all of IAS 1; some paragraphs were retained in IFRS 18, others moved to IAS 8 and IFRS 7
- **The critical framing point:** recognition and measurement are unchanged. Net profit is identical.
- The three pillars: defined subtotals, MPM disclosure, enhanced aggregation and disaggregation
- The complete set of financial statements; the third statement of financial position where there has been retrospective adjustment or reclassification
- Labels: other names may be used for statements, totals, subtotals and line items provided they faithfully represent what they describe
- Effective date, early adoption, and the Ghana position
- **Why this is urgent now:** the comparative-period arithmetic worked through on screen for a 31 December year end

**Activity:** Participants map their own year end to the IFRS 18 timeline and identify the date by which comparative-period categorisation must be complete.

---

### Module 2 — The five categories and the two required subtotals
**1.5 hours**

**Purpose:** The structural core of the standard.

**Topics**
- **The five categories:** operating, investing, financing, income taxes, discontinued operations
- **The two required subtotals:** operating profit, and profit before financing and income taxes
- **Operating as the residual category** — income and expenses not classified elsewhere. Operating profit is now a defined figure, not a management choice.
- **Investing:** income and expenses from investments in associates, joint ventures and unconsolidated subsidiaries; from cash and cash equivalents; and from other assets generating returns individually and largely independently of the entity's other resources
- **Financing:** income and expenses from liabilities arising from transactions involving only the raising of finance, plus interest and interest-rate effects on other liabilities such as lease and pension liabilities
- Income taxes and discontinued operations categories
- Operating expenses by nature, by function, or mixed — and the requirement to choose the presentation giving the most useful structured summary
- Where a function presentation is used, the specified nature information required in the notes
- Additional subtotals: permitted, but must be consistent with the category structure and faithfully labelled
- Other comprehensive income — what remains unchanged

**Activity — category sort:** Participants classify 30 income and expense line items from a realistic set of accounts, including deliberately borderline items. Debrief on where the group split. *Protect this exercise; it is one of two that survive the compressed format.*

---

### Module 3 — Main business activity and classification judgement
**1 hour**

**Purpose:** Where the real difficulty lies. Module 2 gives the rules; this gives the judgement.

**Topics**
- **Main business activities** — the concept, and why it determines classification
- **The two specified activities:**
  - Investing in assets (investment property entities, investment holding companies)
  - Providing financing to customers (banks, lenders, entities with significant customer finance arms)
- How classification shifts: income and expenses that would otherwise sit in investing or financing move to operating
- Entities with more than one main business activity, and cases where the answer is genuinely arguable
- **Ghana application:** banks and rural banks, insurance companies, investment holding structures, entities with in-house customer credit
- **Foreign exchange differences:** classified in the same category as the items they relate to; where impracticable due to excessive cost or effort, classified in operating
- Derivatives and hedging instruments: classification by reference to what is hedged
- Documenting the judgement — what the auditor will ask for

**Activity:** One entity scenario with an ambiguous main business activity. Participants determine classification and articulate the reasoning they would put in the file.

> **Facilitator note:** Resist giving clean answers where the standard does not. The point is that classification is a documented judgement, not a lookup. In the three-day version this module ran 1.5 hours with three scenarios — at one hour, run one scenario properly rather than three superficially.

---

### Module 4 — Management-defined performance measures
**1.5 hours**

**Purpose:** The disclosure change with the largest practical impact. Entities relying heavily on alternative performance measures face the most noticeable effect.

**Topics**
- **The MPM definition** — a subtotal of income and expenses used in public communications outside the financial statements, communicating management's view of an aspect of financial performance, and not a subtotal listed or required by IFRS
- Subtotals specifically excluded from the definition
- What counts as "public communications outside the financial statements": annual report narrative, investor presentations, press releases, lender reporting, social media
- Measures likely to become MPMs: adjusted EBITDA, underlying profit, profit before exceptional items, normalised earnings, adjusted operating profit
- **The single-note requirement** and its contents:
  - A statement that the measure is an MPM and is not necessarily comparable with measures used by other entities
  - Why it provides useful information and how it is calculated
  - Reconciliation to the most directly comparable IFRS-specified subtotal
  - For each reconciling item, the income tax effect and the effect on non-controlling interests
  - Explanation of any change in calculation, or of a measure ceasing to be disclosed
- The governance consequence: a measure the board has used loosely in investor material now carries audited disclosure obligations
- The practical decision: keep the measure and disclose it properly, or stop using it

**Activity — MPM identification:** Participants review an annual report narrative and investor deck for a sample entity and list every measure that would become an MPM. Most groups miss several — that is the lesson. *Protect this exercise.*

---

### Module 5 — Aggregation, disaggregation and labelling
**30 minutes**

**Purpose:** The quietest of the three pillars. At 30 minutes this is principles only — the detailed label audit moves into the Day 2 workshop.

**Topics**
- The principles: aggregate items sharing characteristics; disaggregate items that do not
- The distinct roles of the primary financial statements and the notes — useful structured summary versus material detail
- **Labelling:** items must be described in a way that faithfully represents their characteristics
- **The "other" problem:** where a residual line is labelled "other", its composition must be explained. Most existing financial statements fail this on first review.
- Interaction with IFRS 8 segment reporting
- Interim reporting: IAS 34 requires the same headings and subtotals as the most recent annual financial statements, with the enhanced aggregation and disaggregation principles considered in condensed interim statements

**No standalone activity.** Application is folded into workshop step 6.

---

### Module 6 — Consequential amendments
**1 hour**

**Purpose:** The changes outside IFRS 18 itself that catch people out — particularly the cash flow statement.

**Topics**
- **IAS 7 Statement of Cash Flows:**
  - Under the indirect method, the reconciliation now begins with operating profit rather than profit before income taxes
  - Dividends paid are presented as financing; interest paid is generally classified within financing
  - Cash generation is unaffected — presentation, trend analysis and stakeholder reading are not
  - Practical effect on covenant calculations and historical trend data
- **IAS 8** renamed *Basis of Preparation of Financial Statements*, with paragraphs relocated from IAS 1
- **IAS 33 Earnings per Share:** additional EPS metrics beyond basic and diluted may only be disclosed where the numerator is a total or subtotal specified in IFRS 18, or an MPM
- **IFRS 7** — paragraphs relocated from IAS 1
- **IFRS 19 Subsidiaries without Public Accountability: Disclosures** — also effective 1 January 2027; reduced disclosure regime and who may use it

**Activity — cash flow restatement:** Participants convert a prepared indirect-method cash flow statement to the new starting point, reclassify interest and dividends, and identify which loan covenants would be affected.

> **Facilitator note:** The IFRS Interpretations Committee has an active IFRS 18 work plan and narrow-scope amendments continue to be issued. Check the Committee's current position before each delivery and update this module.

---

### Module 7 — Transition planning
**1 hour**

**Purpose:** Turn understanding into a dated plan.

**Topics**
- Retrospective application in accordance with IAS 8, with restatement of comparatives
- **The relief:** the IAS 8 quantitative disclosures are not required — the adjustment to each line item and to basic and diluted EPS in the current period
- **The requirement:** for the comparative period, a reconciliation between the restated amounts presented and the amounts previously presented under IAS 1
- Early adoption is permitted; entities that early adopt must disclose that fact
- Disclosure of expected effects in the periods before adoption, and what auditors and regulators will expect in FY2026 statements
- **Building the transition plan:**
  - Impact assessment on the statement of profit or loss and the statement of cash flows
  - Determining main business activity and documenting the judgement
  - MPM inventory and board decisions on which measures to retain
  - Chart of accounts: tagging income and expenses to IFRS 18 categories
  - System and reporting-pack changes
  - Consolidation implications where group entities classify differently
  - Communication with lenders, analysts and the audit committee before the numbers move
  - Finance team training
- Working backwards from 31 December 2027 to a dated task list

**Activity:** Participants draft a transition timetable for their own entity, with owners and dates.

**Outcome:** A transition plan they can take to their CFO on Monday.

---

### Module 8 — Implementation workshop
**3 hours**

**Purpose:** The deliverable, and the reason this course is worth more than a technical webinar. Participants restate a real set of accounts rather than watching someone explain how it would be done.

**Working through, using the participant's own trial balance or the supplied dataset:**

1. **Map the chart of accounts** to the five IFRS 18 categories, flagging every item requiring judgement
2. **Determine main business activity** and apply any consequent reclassification
3. **Build the statement of profit or loss**, with both required subtotals and an appropriate expense presentation
4. **Restate the cash flow statement** on the new basis
5. **Prepare the MPM note**, including reconciliation, tax effect and NCI effect
6. **Review labelling and disaggregation**, correcting non-compliant line items
7. **Prepare the transition reconciliation** between restated and previously presented comparatives
8. **Compile the judgement memorandum** documenting each significant classification decision

**Review and close (30 minutes):** Participants present one classification judgement they found difficult and defend it to the room. Facilitator and peers challenge.

**Outcome:** A restated set of primary statements, an MPM note, and a documented judgement file for their own entity.

> **Facilitator note:** This three-hour block is the course. If Day 1 overran, or if Module 6 or 7 is running long, cut from those — never from here. Steps 1–3 are the minimum viable output; steps 4–8 are completed in the room where time allows and finished afterwards using the templates.

## Appendix A — Facilitator notes

1. **Lead with the comparative-period arithmetic.** The single most persuasive thing in the course is showing a 31 December preparer that their comparative year is already running. Do it in the first thirty minutes.
2. **Protect the workshop.** Twelve hours is tight. The Day 2 workshop is the differentiator and the justification for the fee. Cut anything else first.
3. **Enforce the trial balance requirement.** Confirm at registration, remind three days out, and have the supplied dataset genuinely ready. The workshop collapses if half the room is watching.
4. **Do not resolve genuine ambiguity.** Modules 3 and 5 involve judgement. Participants must leave able to reason and document, not able to recall an answer key.
5. **Currency.** The IFRS Interpretations Committee has an active IFRS 18 work plan. Review Modules 3 and 6 before every cohort.
6. **Confirm the Ghana position.** Verify with ICAG whether any local adoption guidance or transitional relief has been issued before advertising the course.
7. **Sector variants.** Banks and insurers have a materially different experience of Module 3. If a cohort is sector-concentrated, rebuild the scenario accordingly.
